02HOW I WORK

AI-native, judgment-first. Here's the honest split.

In 2026, claiming you don't use AI is a credibility problem, and claiming it does your thinking is a bigger one. I've built agentic AI products twice — so I know exactly where the model ends and the PM begins.

Where AI helped

Speed on the boring middle

First drafts of BRDs, KPI docs, and stakeholder updates. AI gets me to 60% in minutes; I spend the saved hours on the 40% that decides outcomes — usually inside raw session data.

Prototyping at conversation speed

Cursor, n8n, and Lovable let me test a flow before asking engineering for anything. Two of my teams' hackathon builds — an agentic farm advisor and a predictive-maintenance platform — shipped working demos this way.

Adversarial review

I paste my own recommendation and ask a model to argue against it. Cheap red team. In the Ather case, this killed two of our own slides before the judges could.

Where judgment overrode it

Rules-first, not LLM-first

Building Fasalsetu.ai, the obvious move was letting an LLM answer farmers directly. We overrode it: LLMs plan and explain, a deterministic rules engine decides. Advice you can't reproduce is advice a farmer can't trust — and a regulator won't either.

The KPI feasibility matrix

At Bajaj, AI happily proposed 20+ metrics for the BLU Bot redesign. Nine survived contact with instrumentation reality. Models optimize for completeness; products die from unmeasurable KPIs.

The boring fix at HiCounselor

Every AI review of the funnel suggested a redesign. Session data said four targeted UX changes. Four changes shipped, conversion rose 18%. Judgment is mostly knowing when the cheap answer is the right one.
